From Engineer to Tech Lead: What Changes and What Doesn’t

Reflecting on my 8-year journey at Pathao — what truly changes when you grow from an engineer into a staff-level technical leader, and what doesn’t.

You won’t stop writing code — but you’ll start writing influence.

After spending 8 years at Pathao, my journey has taken me from being a hands-on software engineer to a Senior Staff Engineer responsible for system design, technical vision, and mentoring teams across the org. This transition wasn’t overnight — it was built step by step, from writing my first microservice, to designing the backbone of business-critical systems.

In this post, I want to share what truly changes when you go from engineer to tech lead (or staff+ level), and what surprisingly doesn’t.

🔄 What Changes#

Scope of Impact#

As an engineer, I focused on solving the task in front of me. But in senior roles, every decision I make — an API design, a tech choice, even naming a service — has ripple effects across multiple teams and products.

When I started leading core services like Authentication, Location, and Supply, I realized that technical decisions weren’t isolated anymore — they had to consider scalability, team adoption, future extensions, and even on-call implications.

More Strategy, Less Hands-on#

The deeper you go, the less you’re judged by the code you write — and more by the code you enable others to write.

I still code. I review. I prototype. But I spend more time on design docs, architecture discussions, grooming long-term tech debt, and aligning with Product, QA, DevOps, and App teams. The real job? Unblocking others and giving them clarity.

Leading Without Authority#

You’re not a manager. You’re a technical leader. And leadership here means influence — not instruction.

You’ll often face disagreements, conflicting priorities, and pushback. That’s okay. Being a good tech lead means listening, aligning, and making trade-offs visible — not just pushing your opinion because of your title.

You Start Multiplying, Not Just Building#

As a Senior Staff Engineer, I’ve realized: my job is to create impact at scale.

That means mentoring engineers, shaping architecture that multiple teams can rely on, and helping others grow. The question isn’t “how fast can I build this?” but “how can I make it easy for others to build this and more?”

From Features to Systems#

You go from delivering a story point to asking, “how does this system behave under load?”, “what happens if X fails?”, “is this design extensible?”

When I worked on PayLater, communication systems, and now cross-cutting platform services, my mindset shifted: build resilient systems, not just ship features.

⚓ What Doesn’t Change#

Fundamentals Are Forever#

Clean code, SOLID principles, observability, testability — none of that becomes “optional” when you grow. In fact, you’re the one enforcing and championing them.

Whether I’m debugging a flaky Redis interaction or advising on API versioning, I lean heavily on the same principles I followed as a junior engineer.

You’re Still in the Trenches#

Leadership doesn’t mean detachment. I stay close to the codebase. Not because I don’t trust others, but because context matters — and being hands-on helps me make better architectural decisions.

Sometimes I write code. Sometimes I jump in to investigate weird memory leaks. Staying grounded keeps me connected to reality.

You Keep Learning#

I’ve learned more in the last two years than I did in the four before that — not just tech, but people, process, trade-offs, and strategy.

Being a Staff+ engineer is humbling. You constantly realize how big the system is — and how much more there is to master.

Ownership Never Gets Old#

From building Pathao Wallet early on to leading services that power our platform today — ownership has been a constant theme.

It’s not just owning uptime or fixing bugs. It’s owning the outcome, even if it’s messy, cross-functional, or way outside your comfort zone.

Final Thoughts#

The leap from engineer to tech lead or staff engineer isn’t a linear promotion. It’s a mindset shift:

  • From building things fast to building things that last.
  • From “how do I solve this?” to “how does the team solve this better together?”
  • From individual contributor to impact multiplier.

Not everyone needs to follow this path. And that’s perfectly fine. Great engineers come in all shapes and titles. But if you’re on the path — or just started walking it — know that the learning never stops, and neither does the fun.
